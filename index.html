Welcome to the MBTA Project

Throughout our time as Boston students, we have experienced many issues with Boston’s transportation system. From unreliable predictions on T and bus arrivals to waiting upwards of 30 minutes for the next bus to arrive, Boston students are all too familiar with the uncertainty surrounding public transit. Our visualizations aim to discover the distribution of predicted versus actual arrival times across Boston, find hotspots of inefficiency, and prevent the pain of stranding ourselves. These visualizations will allow students to confidently leave Snell that extra crucial minute later, or even avoid trouble spots entirely, opting for more sure routes.

Using our identified datasets, we aim to map how often the mass dot website accurately predicts the true arrival time of the MBTA Orange Line. Additionally, we will aim to graph how many minutes, on average, the Mass DOT predicted time is off from the true departure time. Altogether, the end goal is to make a cohesive and comprehensive visualization for students and frequent commuters to understand Orange Line behaviors.

Data Introduction

The data we decided to use was collected by the Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority. The MBTA, which provides public transportation across Massachusetts, collects this data to further improve its services, and due to its government relations and knowledge of its own operations, we believe that this is a credible source. We obtained our data from the MBTA website at the following source. We made sure that the data was able to be loaded and currently contains 9672 rows of data, though it is continuously updating and likely to surpass this during our research. We’ve noted that the data is collected on a weekly basis for each line and mode of transport, allowing for insights to be drawn on a weekly basis. This accounts for half of our visualizations.

——

Another dataset we ended up using was one we created ourselves. Pulled from the MBTA V3 API, it combines the GTFS static schedule data (the planned arrival and departure times) with the GTFS-Realtime data to get the real-time arrival and departure times. This way, we can directly compare the differences and check the MBTA line accuracy. This allows us to compare which locations are consistently facing slowdowns and see which days and seasons tend to have worse MBTA activity. We’re focusing on the Orange line to narrow down our scope from the massive dataset. Something unique about this approach is that we can pull up-to-date, real-world data rather than relying on historical archives, which may no longer be relevant. This accounts for the other half of our visualizations.
